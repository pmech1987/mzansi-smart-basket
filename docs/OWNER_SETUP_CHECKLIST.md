# Owner setup checklist — keep Mzansi Smart Basket under your control

Use this order so you do not pay for a domain, company name or Play account before checking the name and ownership.

## A. Confirm the brand and code ownership

- Search the proposed name `Mzansi Smart Basket` on the CIPC company-name search and check for similar registered trade marks. Also check matching `.co.za` and other domains. The ZIP does not prove that the name is available.
- Confirm who wrote the original application and whether they assigned the source code, design and other deliverables to you. Keep the original contract, invoices and written IP assignment/licence.
- If a freelancer, friend or company supplied code, get a signed document saying exactly which rights transfer to you or your company. Payment alone should not be treated as proof that copyright has transferred.
- Do not add retailer logos, pamphlet artwork, product photographs or third-party source content without the necessary licence.

## B. Register the company (when ready)

1. Decide the company name and whether you want to reserve it first. A CIPC name reservation is currently R50 when submitted electronically; BizPortal currently advertises a private company registration at R125 without a name or R175 including a name. Recheck the amount at checkout.
2. Open [BizPortal](https://www.bizportal.gov.za/) and create/sign in to the account using an email address and phone number you control.
3. Choose **Company Registration** and follow the private company (Pty) Ltd path. Use the correct identity details for each director/shareholder. If you are the sole founder, you can structure it so you hold all the shares, subject to the MOI and correct filings.
4. Keep the registration certificate, company number, MOI and proof of who holds the shares. Do not assume the app's copyright automatically moves to the company.
5. Submit the beneficial-ownership information and keep the securities/share register accurate. File the annual return and beneficial-ownership information within the CIPC deadline.
6. Check the SARS tax profile/eFiling access. Ask an accountant about company income tax, provisional tax, VAT/Turnover Tax, record keeping and what expenses can be claimed.
7. Open a separate business bank account. Pay app expenses from the company account once the company owns or operates the business; keep invoices and statements.

## C. Put the app assets in the right owner account

Create/secure these accounts using an email address you control:

- GitHub: private source repository, two-factor authentication and recovery codes.
- Cloudflare: Pages project, DNS and deployment access.
- Domain registrar: registered to you or, preferably, the company once established; enable auto-renew and two-factor authentication.
- Gemini: the API account and billing/usage limits; store its API key only as a GitHub Actions secret.
- Google Play Console: choose personal or organisation account deliberately. If the company will own and publish the app, an organisation account is normally the cleaner long-term arrangement.

For every account, record the owner, recovery email/phone, billing method, renewal date and any authorised collaborators. Do not let a developer register the app under their personal Google Play account if the company is meant to own and publish it.

## D. Transfer/assign the app to the company

Once the company exists, have an attorney or IP professional prepare an assignment/licence that identifies the source code, designs, documentation, domain, app name, database/feed schema and other deliverables. Move account ownership or transfer the relevant assets to the company using the provider's supported process. Keep a signed copy and an asset register. An app store listing, GitHub repository or domain registration is not, on its own, proof of copyright ownership.

## E. Do not launch publicly until these gates are complete

- Demo prices removed or still clearly labelled as demo.
- Written basis/permission for every brochure/source used commercially.
- Human review of every published offer and expiry date.
- Real privacy and terms pages, operator identity, support contact and POPIA process.
- Physical Android device tests, signed release key and Play Console policy declarations.
- Error-report/correction process and a plan to keep offers fresh.
