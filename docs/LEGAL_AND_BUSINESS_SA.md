# South African launch, copyright and business checklist

This is practical general information, not a legal opinion. Before using retailer materials commercially, ask a South African IP/media lawyer to review the exact sources, website terms, extraction method and planned display.

## Free pamphlets, factual offers and source permissions

A retailer distributing a pamphlet for free makes the physical or digital copy free to receive or view. That does not, by itself, grant a recipient a general commercial licence to reproduce the pamphlet, artwork, photographs, page design or a substantial part of the retailer's curated offer compilation in another app. The retailer's advertising purpose is relevant commercial context, but it is not blanket permission for a third party to republish the advertisement. The precise rights holder, content, amount used, source terms, access method and any licence or statutory exception matter.

A price-comparison product should distinguish **individual facts** from **protected expression and compilations**. A single product/price/date fact is not ordinarily protected merely because it appears in a pamphlet. However, South Africa's Copyright Act includes written tables and compilations as literary works; a compilation can qualify if it is original. South African decisions have recognised copyright in price lists/directories and in substantial compilations assembled through skill, labour or effort. Do not assume that the fact-only rule permits bulk copying a retailer's or catalogue publisher's complete selection or database. Independently collect and verify individual offers, use your own schema and presentation, and avoid lifting another publisher's database or substantial curated selection. This is a risk-reduction approach, not a guarantee that a particular extraction is lawful.

A lower-risk display is a new, independently structured offer record: retailer name in plain text; product description; brand and pack size; advertised price; loyalty price and conditions; validity dates; region/branch restriction; timestamp checked; and a link to an authorised source page. Do not copy full flyer pages, price-tag artwork, product photographs, logos, distinctive design or layout unless you have written permission or a lawyer confirms a specific legal basis. Attribution is useful for transparency but is not, by itself, a copyright licence.

**OCR is a tool, not a legal permission.** OCR can convert text from a document into candidate data, but it does not change the copyright status of the source or the rights in a table/compilation. Uploading a publicly accessible PDF is not automatically authorisation for commercial extraction. Process local PDFs/images only where the operator has documented a valid basis for that processing and intended commercial use. For a remote source, check its current terms and access rules before automating downloads. Do not bypass logins, paywalls, rate limits, CAPTCHAs, technical controls or anti-bot restrictions. Do not treat a user's upload as proof that the user has rights to it.

The app should use retailer names only to identify where a listed offer is advertised, in a way that does not suggest sponsorship or affiliation. The Trade Marks Act contains a bona fide descriptive-use provision, but that is not a blanket permission to use logos or create a misleading brand presentation. Keep retailer names in ordinary text, avoid retailer logos unless licensed, and state prominently that the app is independent and not endorsed by listed retailers.

Shoprite-specific caution: a Shoprite Checkers promotional-rules PDF located during this review says that information on the relevant website/digital channels/till slip may be used to learn about products/services but not reused commercially, and that automated search/download technology may not be used to access that promotion site without prior written consent. This is a promotion-specific document, not proof of the terms governing every Shoprite pamphlet or every Shoprite web service. The live Shoprite terms page should be reviewed for the actual source being used. Apply the same source-by-source check to Checkers, Pick n Pay, Boxer, SPAR, Woolworths, catalogue aggregators and affiliate feeds.

Before commercial ingestion, keep a source register recording: source owner/publisher; exact URL or document; retrieval date; current terms version; whether automated download is permitted; whether commercial data extraction is permitted; whether images/logos may be displayed; permission/licence/evidence reference; reviewer; and expiry/review date. If the source terms are silent or unclear, do not assume permission—obtain written approval or South African IP advice. A permission should specify fields, images/logos (if any), territory, duration, update frequency, attribution, sublicensing and whether monetisation is allowed.

## Copyright law status (checked 25 November 2026)

The current baseline remains the Copyright Act 98 of 1978, together with applicable court orders and amendments actually in force. On 26 June 2026, the Constitutional Court ruled on the President's referral concerning the Copyright Amendment Bill. The Court held that proposed section 12A (fair use) and several other referred clauses survived the specific constitutional objections before it, but found proposed educational exceptions in section 12D(1)-(5) unconstitutional. The judgment did not itself enact the Bill. Parliament's bill page still identifies the bill as referred to the Constitutional Court/sent for assent, and a 20 November 2026 report on the Department's committee briefing says Parliament must revise the Bill to remedy the unconstitutional clauses before enactment. Do not rely on proposed section 12A as a current statutory permission until the legislation is duly enacted and commenced. Ask an IP lawyer to assess whether an existing Copyright Act exception applies to the exact use you plan.

## Consumer-facing price accuracy

The Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008 prohibits false, misleading or deceptive marketing representations and includes specific provisions on bait marketing and the availability of advertised offers. Those standards are important when designing a commercial comparison service; have counsel assess the exact obligations applicable to the app operator. The app should:

- visibly label demo, unreviewed, cached and human-reviewed prices;
- show source, date, region and loyalty-card conditions where available;
- compare the same brand/size/quantity where possible, or calculate a comparable unit price;
- avoid saying “cheapest in South Africa” unless the comparison is genuinely comprehensive and current;
- never guarantee stock, branch availability or retailer acceptance;
- provide a correction/removal contact and promptly fix material errors.

The Advertising Regulatory Board's Code also expects price comparisons to be factual, verifiable and not misleading. Check the current Code when advertising the app or publishing paid comparison claims.

## POPIA and privacy

The current app stores basket/settings/loyalty-card entries locally and only requests device location after a user taps GPS. The app code uses the coordinates to estimate a province and does not save or send the coordinates to the price-feed API. Hosting providers and external websites can still process IP addresses and request logs. Before launch, publish a complete POPIA notice with the actual responsible party, contact details, purposes, recipients, retention, security, rights process and any analytics/ad/affiliate processors. Revisit the notice before introducing accounts, cloud sync, analytics, push notifications or targeted advertising.

## Registering the business

A practical structure for a one-founder commercial app is usually a private company (Pty) Ltd, but registration is not a substitute for contracts, IP ownership or bookkeeping. BizPortal currently lists R125 for company registration without a name and R175 including a name; CIPC's fee page lists online name reservation at R50. Check the current checkout amount before payment.

Suggested sequence:

1. Search the business name at CIPC and check whether a matching trade mark or confusingly similar app/domain already exists. Company-name approval is not trade-mark protection.
2. Register a private company on the official CIPC/BizPortal platform. Use your own contact details and store all access credentials securely.
3. Keep the CIPC registration certificate, memorandum of incorporation, company number and shareholding record.
4. Submit the required beneficial-ownership information and keep it current. CIPC says annual returns must be filed with beneficial-ownership information within 30 business days after the company's anniversary date.
5. Confirm the company's SARS eFiling profile and tax types. CIPC says companies are automatically registered for income tax through the CIPC-SARS interface, but the owner must still check the SARS profile and meet filing/payment duties.
6. Open a separate company bank account, keep invoices/receipts, record income/expenses and do not mix company funds with personal spending.
7. Put the app's domain, GitHub repository, Cloudflare account, API keys, Google Play developer account and any future retailer/affiliate contracts under your control. If a developer or designer contributes, sign an IP assignment/licence agreement so the company has the rights it needs.
8. Ask an accountant about provisional tax, income tax, VAT and whether Turnover Tax is available for the company's actual circumstances.

## VAT point as at 25 November 2026

SARS is administering the announced thresholds from 1 April 2026: compulsory VAT registration at R2.3 million of taxable supplies in a consecutive 12-month period and voluntary registration generally available above R120,000, subject to conditions. SARS notes that the associated amendments had not yet been promulgated when its Budget 2026 FAQ was published, but that it was administering applications on the new threshold. These amounts refer to taxable supplies, not profit. Monitor rolling 12-month supplies and ask an accountant before the threshold is approached.

## Ownership and account security

- Create a GitHub account using an email address you control; enable two-factor authentication and save recovery codes.
- Keep the repository under your account or an organisation you own/control. Give contractors least-privilege access and remove access when work ends.
- Put `GEMINI_API_KEY` in GitHub Actions Secrets only. Never put it in `index.html`, JSON files or mobile app code; anything bundled into an APK/AAB can be extracted.
- Own and renew the domain using an account in your control. Enable registrar two-factor authentication and auto-renewal.
- Use a password manager and keep two encrypted backups of the Android signing key. Losing the Play signing credentials can complicate updates.
- For full public distribution, Google currently lists a one-time US$25 developer registration fee. An organisation account requires organisation identity details, a website and a D‑U‑N‑S number; the D‑U‑N‑S application itself is free. New personal accounts created after 13 November 2023 must complete a closed test with at least 12 opted-in testers for 14 continuous days before applying for production access. An organisation account may be the better fit once the Pty Ltd and its details are ready; do not register an account using someone else's identity.

## Can the app earn money?

It can, but earnings are uncertain and depend on repeat usage, trusted accurate data and conversion. The sensible order is: prove weekly use; then ask retailers/affiliate networks for written referral terms; then consider ads; only later test premium functions such as alerts, saved price history and ad-free use. Do not promise affiliate income until a programme accepts the app and confirms commission, attribution, payment thresholds and prohibited traffic.

Use this calculation rather than guessing: **monthly revenue = approved affiliate conversions × commission + monetised ad impressions ÷ 1,000 × actual ad RPM + paid subscriptions − operating costs − tax.** Measure real conversions and costs before projecting income. Hosting may be low-cost at the beginning, but extraction API usage, legal review, support, accounting, domain renewals and advertising can create expenses.
